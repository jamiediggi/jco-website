The Framework

The Perfect Customer Journey

A simple 6-stage framework that turns strangers into fans — and fans into your best source of new business.

Get the Free Guide Get Your Blueprint

Why most marketing doesn't work

Let's be honest: most small businesses struggle with marketing. Not because they're lazy. Not because they don't try.

They're doing the work — posting on social media, updating the website, sending emails when they remember. But nothing connects. Growth feels random. Leads come in dribs and drabs. And every month feels like starting from scratch.

The problem isn't the tactics. It's the lack of a journey.

Without a clear path for your audience to follow, every marketing activity is disconnected. You're building pieces that don't fit together. You're asking strangers to marry you on the first date.

The Science

The science behind it

Marketing tactics change every year. Algorithms shift. Platforms rise and fall.

But one thing hasn't changed in thousands of years: humans.

Whatever you're selling, your buyers are human beings. And human beings don't go from stranger to customer in one step. They need a journey.

In the 1960s, zoologist Desmond Morris identified 12 stages of human intimacy — the steps people naturally go through when building a relationship. Skip too many steps and it feels wrong. Pushy. Uncomfortable.

The same is true in business.

The Perfect Customer Journey is built on this principle. It maps the natural progression from "never heard of you" to "telling everyone about you." Six stages. Each one the logical next step from the last.

It's simpler than you think. And it works.

1 Stage One

Get Noticed

You: Get visible to the right people
They: Discover you exist

You can't sell to people who don't know you exist. This stage is about showing up where your ideal customers are — with a message that makes them pay attention.

This isn't about being everywhere. It's about being in the right places, with the right message, for the right people.

Visibility

Be seen by the people who need you most

2 Stage Two

Connect

You: Get permission to follow up
They: Give you their details

Attention is worthless if it disappears. This stage is about turning a moment of interest into an ongoing relationship.

You offer something valuable — a guide, a tool, a resource — in exchange for permission to stay in touch. Now you're not relying on algorithms. You can reach them directly.

Permission

Turn attention into a relationship

3 Stage Three

Nurture

You: Build trust and demonstrate value
They: Start to believe you can help

Just because someone gave you their email doesn't mean they trust you. Not yet.

This stage is about proving your worth. Showing up consistently. Helping before you ask for anything. Building the kind of trust that makes buying feel like the obvious next step.

Trust

Prove your value before you ask for anything

4 Stage Four

Convert

You: Ask for the sale
They: Become a paying customer

This is the moment of truth. But if you've done the previous stages well, it shouldn't feel like a hard sell.

You're not pushing. You're offering the next logical step to someone who already trusts you. Clear offer. Clear price. Clear outcome.

Sale

The natural next step, not a hard sell

5 Stage Five

Deliver & Wow

You: Exceed expectations
They: Feel they made the right choice

Getting the sale isn't the finish line. It's the starting line.

This is where you make them feel like they made the best decision of their life. Over-deliver. Exceed expectations. Make them think, "If this is what I get, imagine what else they can do for me."

Delight

Make them feel like a genius for choosing you

6 Stage Six

Create Fans

You: Turn customers into referrers
They: Tell others about you

Happy customers are willing to recommend you. But they usually won't unless you ask.

This stage is about making it easy for them to spread the word. Ask for reviews. Set up referral programmes. Give them something to share. When your customers are actively sending others your way, you've built something sustainable.

Advocacy

A system that feeds itself with referrals

The Key Insight

It's not a funnel. It's a loop.

Most people think of marketing as a funnel — pour people in at the top, some come out the bottom as customers. Then start again.

The Perfect Customer Journey is different.

When Stage 6 works properly, your fans feed back into Stage 1. Their referrals become your new awareness. Their testimonials build trust for new prospects.

The journey becomes self-sustaining.

That's not just good marketing. That's a business that grows without burning you out.

How to use this framework

The Perfect Customer Journey isn't complicated. But it does require honesty about where you are now.

Audit your current journey

For each of the 6 stages, ask yourself: what do I have in place here? Write down what you're currently doing at each stage. If you've got nothing, that's a gap.

Find the biggest gap

Where is your journey broken? Where are you losing people? That's where to focus first.

Fix one thing

Don't try to build the whole journey at once. Pick the biggest gap and fix it. Then move to the next one.

It's simpler than you think. The hard part is being honest about what's missing.
